Reference recordings
How to record a good reference
  • Quiet room. Turn off fans, music and TV. Soft furnishings help; a bare room echoes.
  • Same microphone position each time, about a hand-span (20–30 cm) from your mouth and slightly to the side, so breaths don't pop.
  • Speak naturally, at your normal pace and volume, as if talking to someone. Don't perform unless you want the clone to perform.
  • 8–15 seconds of continuous speech works best (at least 3 s and at most 30 s once silence is trimmed).
  • Watch the level meter. Keep the peaks below the red zone; if it turns red you're clipping: move back or turn the input gain down.
  • Record several (different sentences, maybe different moods), then generate the same text from each and keep the one that sounds most like you.
  • Type exactly what you said as the transcript, word for word. A wrong transcript is the most common cause of poor cloning.

Something to read, if you want: “I moved the meeting to Thursday because the projector in the small room still isn't working. If that's a problem, call me back and we'll sort it out.”

Advanced

Long text is read in chunks of about 300 bytes, each limited to 600 frames (≈ 28 s of audio); each chunk is conditioned on your reference and the previous chunk. Defaults are the model developer's published settings (temperature 1.0, top-p 0.9, top-k 30).

Takes
Every take is kept with its text, voice, recording, settings, seed and model revision. “Same seed again” replays a take exactly, byte for byte, on this runtime.

Everything here runs on this Mac (127.0.0.1). Recordings, transcripts and takes stay in a private folder on this Mac and are never sent anywhere.
